# Brief for a blind judge of the Ultra study: Apple Intelligence

The owner asked (04/11/2026) whether the request improvements found for Cue's cloud writer also help the writer that runs on the iPhone
(Apple Intelligence, on the device or on Apple's Private Cloud Compute), and at which length it writes best inside the networks' 1:15 to
2:30. Each packet is one case: the request and several scripts for it, under random letters. You are one of three independent judges.

## Rules
1. Everything in `tools/ultra/study/judge-brief.md` applies (blindness, reading every script before scoring, one JSON per packet in the
   rubric's shape, ranking with no ties, reporting back only which packets you finished). Read it and `tools/ultra/study/rubric.md` first,
   entirely. Never try to guess which setting wrote a script.
2. The context shows what the app sent the writer and the writer's standing instructions, where the creator's voice is (who they are, how
   they speak, their rules and their usual ending). Judge Voice against those.
3. **Stage cues** in square brackets (`[pause]`, `[smile]`, `[look at camera]`) are directions the teleprompter shows apart; they are not
   spoken and the word counts leave them out. Ignore them, except when they are so many or so odd that they clutter the read or replace
   spoken words: then take it off **Naturalness** once.
4. **Length:** each script's header gives the words it is judged against. Some scripts were written without a length (the request called
   the platform's duration informational); the header then gives the words the creator expects for this video, and the Length anchors
   apply to those words all the same: a script far below them is a short draft the creator must grow.
5. These writers are smaller than the cloud's. Use the rubric's anchors as they are, without grading on a curve: a 5 means the same thing
   here as anywhere. Real failures (broken sentences, a block that repeats another, an ending that asks for something twice, the wrong
   language, an English word in a Portuguese or Spanish script that creators there would not say) are scored where the rubric puts them.
